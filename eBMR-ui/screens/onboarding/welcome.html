Welcome to NZ-eBMR
Electronic batch manufacturing records

Your workspace is empty — and that is correct.

NZ-eBMR does not ship with sample products, made-up materials or placeholder users. A regulated record can only be trusted if every value in it was deliberately entered by a named person. So on day one there is nothing here, and the first job is to describe your operation to the system.

This takes an administrator roughly half a day for one site. You do it once.

It replaces the paper batch record

The same document, the same steps, the same signatures — but it refuses to let the wrong thing be written down.

Nothing can be quietly changed

Every entry is attributed, time-stamped and permanent. A correction is a new entry with a reason, never an erasure.

It stops work that must not proceed

Expired material, an uncalibrated balance, an untrained operator — the system blocks the step and says exactly why.

Before anyone can run a batch, seven things must exist
In this order — each step depends on the one above it
0 of 7 done
1 Company & sites Where you manufacture 2 Users Who can sign in 3 Roles & permissions What each person may do 4 Products What you make 5 Materials What goes into it 6 Bill of materials How much of each 7 Equipment & rooms What you make it with

Why the order matters: you cannot assign a user to a site that does not exist, cannot write a bill of materials before the materials exist, and cannot start a batch before there is equipment to make it on. The system enforces this — later steps stay locked until their prerequisites are in place.

One decision to make first: which profiles you run

A profile is the regulatory world a product lives in. It decides what a batch is called, what a problem is called, who may release it, and which records the system demands. Profiles never blend — a product belongs to exactly one.

Medical Device

21 CFR 820 / ISO 13485. Device History Records, serialised units, nonconformances.

Pharmaceutical

21 CFR 210/211. Batch records, yield reconciliation, deviations, Quality Unit release.

Combination Product

21 CFR Part 4. Both rule-sets at once, plus final assembly and a single release authority.

You can run all three in one organisation, as Acme does — each site works in its own profile.
