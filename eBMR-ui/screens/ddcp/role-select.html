This picks a persona so the interface behaves the way permission-based access control actually works — each person sees their own work and cannot reach another person's controls. It's a demo of the effect, not a real login.

Why five different people, not one

The recipe is written by one team, approved by another, issued as an order by a third, executed by a fourth, and released by whoever approved it in the first place — but never by whoever performed it. The same person cannot create an order and also release it, or author a recipe and also approve it, or perform a step and also verify it. That's the two-person rule, and it only means something if the system actually enforces who is signed in.
